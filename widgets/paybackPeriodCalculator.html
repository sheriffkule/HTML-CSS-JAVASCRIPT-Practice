About Payback Period
How to Use
Limitations

The payback period is the time it takes for an investment to generate enough cash flows to recover the initial investment cost.

There are two main types:

  • Simple Payback Period: Doesn't consider the time value of money.
  • Discounted Payback Period: Accounts for the time value of money by discounting future cash flows.

Key characteristics:

  • Shorter payback periods are generally preferred as they indicate faster recovery of investment.
  • Often used as a preliminary screening tool before more sophisticated analysis.
  • Doesn't consider cash flows beyond the paybacks period.

To use this calculator:

  1. Enter your initial investment amount.
  2. If you want to calculate discounted payback period, enter a discount rate (set to 0 for simple payback)
  3. Select whether your annual cash flows are equal or vary each year.
  4. Enter your cash flow amounts.
  5. Click "Calculate" to see result.

Tips:

  • For unequal cash flows, use the "+" button to add more years.
  • You can click "Reset" to clear all inputs.
  • Hover over information icons for additional explanations.

While useful, payback period has several limitations:

  • Ignores time value of money (except in discounted version).
  • Doesn't consider cash flows after payback, potentially favoring short-term projects over more profitable long-term nes.
  • No clear decision criterion, only recovery time.
  • Doesn't measure profitability, only recovery time.

For better investment decisions, consider using payback period alongside other metrics like:

  • Net Present Value (NPV)
  • Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
  • Profitability Index
